# Finite Weil generalized spectrum

Status: **FINITE-BASIS NORMALIZED NUMERICAL RESTRICTION / NOT WEIL POSITIVITY**

This layer normalizes the raw finite Weil pairing matrix from the preceding increment by an independently computed positive Gram matrix.

## Pairing matrix

For the compact boundary-admissible basis `h_j = Q g_j`, let

```text
A_ij = psi(h_i^* * h_j).
```

The matrix `A` is the source-functional restriction already implemented by `weil_quadratic_matrix`.

Its inertia is invariant under invertible changes of basis, but its ordinary eigenvalue magnitudes depend on how the basis vectors are scaled. Consequently, a small ordinary `lambda_min(A)` cannot by itself be interpreted as an intrinsic near-null direction in a function-space norm.

## Multiplicative Gram matrix

This increment declares the numerical normalization

```text
G_ij = integral_0^infinity h_i(rho) h_j(rho) d^*rho,
d^*rho = d rho / rho.
```

Because all basis vectors have the same compact support, the implementation evaluates this integral in logarithmic coordinates with the existing Gauss--Legendre quadrature.

The positive definiteness of `G` is checked numerically. A non-positive Gram eigenvalue is a hard failure; it is not regularized away.

This `L^2(d^*rho)` Gram matrix is an explicit finite numerical normalization. It is **not** identified with the source semilocal Hilbert space `L^2(X_S)`.

## Generalized eigenproblem

For positive-definite `G`, the implementation forms

```text
B = G^(-1/2) A G^(-1/2)
```

using a self-adjoint eigendecomposition of `G`, then diagonalizes the symmetrized numerical representation of `B`.

The resulting values are the generalized eigenvalues of

```text
A v = lambda G v.
```

Unlike the ordinary spectrum of the coefficient matrix `A`, these generalized eigenvalues are invariant under invertible diagonal rescaling of the finite basis. The implementation includes an explicit synthetic regression for that invariance.

The audit reports:

- the raw Weil pairing matrix audit;
- all Gram eigenvalues;
- the Gram condition number;
- all generalized eigenvalues;
- the smallest generalized eigenvalue;
- the numerical asymmetry generated by the whitening multiplication before final symmetrization.

## Manufactured regression

For the existing compact support `(1/2, 7/2)` and the first four Legendre-weighted `Qg_j` basis functions, an independent translation of the formulas produced a minimum generalized eigenvalue near

```text
3.7073077556e-3.
```

The Rust test uses this only as a cross-implementation regression target. Passing the regression confirms agreement of two finite numerical implementations; it is not a theorem and does not establish positivity beyond the tested span.

## Scientific boundary

A positive generalized spectrum on a finite basis means only that the implemented Weil form is numerically positive on that finite span relative to the declared positive norm.

It does not establish:

- positivity on all admissible compact test functions;
- completeness or density of the Legendre-bump family;
- uniform control as the basis dimension grows;
- the semilocal trace formula on `L^2(X_S)`;
- Conjecture 4.1;
- the Riemann hypothesis.

A stable negative generalized eigenvalue would be a candidate finite negative direction and would require independent verification before interpretation.

The next useful numerical layer is a reproducible dimension sweep reporting both raw and generalized minimum eigenvalues, Gram conditioning, boundary residuals, pairing asymmetry and quadrature dependence without assuming their sign.
